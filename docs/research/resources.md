# Official challenge resources (released 13 Nov 2026)

Challenge: *Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions*. See also `problem-statements` and `transcript.txt`.

**Overall:** these are knowledge pages, not datasets. No downloads, APIs, thresholds or device specs on any of them. They tell us *what* to monitor and *why*; we supply the measurements.

## 1. NASA HRP: Five Hazards (nasa.gov/hrp/hazards)
- **Space radiation**
- **Isolation and confinement**
- **Distance from Earth:** Mars is about 140 million miles away, roughly 3 years round trip, versus a 3-day lunar trip. Communication delay, no quick return, little medical help.
- **Gravity fields:** Mars gravity is 3/8 of Earth's, possibly for up to 2 years. Transitions between gravity levels affect balance, bones, heart and fluids.
- **Hostile closed environments:** temperature, pressure, lighting, noise, microbes, immune response.

## 2. NASA HRP: 17 Risks (nasa.gov/hrp/risks)
**Physiology (11)**
- Altered immune response
- Sensorimotor and vestibular (balance) changes
- Cardiovascular adaptations
- Decompression sickness
- Host-microorganism interactions
- Hypoxia (from spacewalk pre-breathe)
- Inadequate food and nutrition
- Spacewalk (EVA) injury
- Reduced cardiorespiratory and musculoskeletal fitness
- **SANS** (eye and brain fluid shift): *"currently NASA's leading spaceflight-related health risk"*
- Bone changes

**Behavioral (5)**
- Behavioral and psychiatric conditions
- Inadequate teamwork
- **Inadequate sleep and irregular schedules**
- Inadequate systems/operations support
- Injury from dynamic loads

**Radiation (1)**
- Radiation-induced cancer

## 3. NASA HRP: Ongoing research (nasa.gov/hrp/ongoing-hrp-research)
**Artemis II**
- **ARCHeR:** wrist actigraphy recording motion, sleep and light exposure.
- **Immune Biomarkers:** blood and saliva samples.
- **Standard Measures.**

**Low Earth orbit**
- **B-Complex:** daily B vitamins for brain and eye changes.
- **Brain Fluid Pressure.**
- **CIPHER:** multi-system study.
- **Manual piloting simulation.**
- **Spacecraft Occupant Protection.**
- **Spinal Fluid Biomarkers.**
- **Thigh Cuff:** keeps fluid in the legs to protect the eyes.
- **Venous Haemostasis:** blood flow and clotting.
- **Zero T2:** exercise without a treadmill.

**Past**
- **Tempus Pro:** a telemedicine device that collects health data for near-real-time remote diagnosis.
- **Food Acceptability.**
- **Omics Archive.**
- **Veggie.**

## 4. CSA: Monitoring radiation and astronaut health (26 Feb 2026)
- **CANS** (Canadian Active Neutron Spectrometer): a compact, autonomous instrument that measures neutron exposure. CSA awarded a $5.5M contract to Bubble Technology Industries.
- **Neutrons are about 30% of total ISS radiation** and "especially harmful."
- **Earlier Canadian instruments:** bubble detectors, MOSFET, EVARM, Radi-N and Radi-N2.
- **Earth applications:** cancer therapy, aircrew protection, public safety.

## 5. CSA: Why we do science in space
- **Moon and Mars bring more of everything:** weightlessness, radiation, isolation, communication delay, less resupply and medical aid.
- **Weightlessness is "similar to accelerated aging"** and to a sedentary lifestyle. Research on bones, heart, blood vessels and brain feeds into Earth medicine: balance, osteoporosis, cardiovascular disease, Type-2 diabetes.
- *"Studying the human body in space for six months gives us data that would take years to gather on Earth."*
